Guide · Visa requirements

Traveling on a Latin American passport while living in the U.S.

If you live in the United States on a Peruvian, Colombian, Mexican or other passport, visa requirements depend on your passport and the destination. This guide sums it up by region.

The basic rule: your passport decides, not your residency

When you travel outside the United States, the destination country looks at your passport, not your Green Card or U.S. visa. Your U.S. status lets you return, but it does not change the destination's entry requirements. That is why two people living in the same city can face different procedures depending on nationality.

There is one common exception: some countries waive the visa for holders of a valid Green Card or U.S. visa (for example Mexico and several Caribbean and Central American countries, for transit or tourism). We confirm this case by case.

Europe (Schengen Area)

No Schengen visa needed (up to 90 days) for passports of Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Honduras, Panama, Paraguay, Peru and Uruguay, among others. From 2025 these travelers must obtain ETIAS, an electronic authorization — not a visa.

Schengen visa required for passports of Bolivia, Cuba, Ecuador, El Salvador, Nicaragua, the Dominican Republic and Venezuela, among others. It is applied for at the consulate of the first destination country and takes 15 to 30 business days. Altamira prepares the supporting paperwork: detailed itinerary, hotel confirmations and insurance.

Egypt, Turkey and Jordan

  • Egypt: visa on arrival or e-Visa (about US$40) for nearly all Latin American passports and the U.S. passport.
  • Turkey: most Latin American passports need no visa for up to 90 days; some require a simple online e-Visa.
  • Jordan: visa on arrival, often included or simplified within organized tours.

Asia (Japan, Thailand, others)

Japan requires no tourist visa (up to 90 days) for Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Costa Rica and several South American countries. Thailand waives the visa for many Latin American passports on short stays. Other Asian countries use e-Visa or visa on arrival. Each itinerary is reviewed before booking.

Important: visa requirements change. This guide is general orientation; the official information is set by each consulate. When we quote your trip we review your specific passport and destination and tell you exactly what applies.
